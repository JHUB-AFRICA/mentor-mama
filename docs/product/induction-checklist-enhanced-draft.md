# Induction checklist: enhanced draft for clinical review

Status: **DRAFT for review by the project's clinical leads.** Not published. The concept-note default (8 items) is unchanged and remains version 1 for every new programme.

Where it lives in the product: Programme Administrator → Induction checklist → Start a draft → _Start from the enhanced draft_. The same content is held as data in `backend/apps/induction/enhanced_draft.py` (`ENHANCED_LABOUR_WARD_CHECKLIST`). Nothing is published until a Programme Administrator reviews, edits and publishes it.

## What this checklist is, and is not

- It is an **orientation** checklist for a student midwife or nurse's first shift on a labour ward: workflow, safety, reporting lines, emergency arrangements, respectful maternity care, documentation and confidentiality, and learning objectives.
- It contains **no patient-specific clinical decisions** and **no clinical protocols**. It points students to the facility's own written protocols.
- It carries the MentorMAMA principle: **no patient-identifiable data is ever recorded in MentorMAMA**, including in item notes, reflections and concerns. The editor also refuses wording that asks for patient names or identifiers.
- It is a starting point. Final wording, ownership of each item and facility-specific items are decisions for the clinical leads (see open questions).

**Size:** 8 sections, 40 items (35 required, 5 optional).

How to read the tables: _Who_ is the person expected to complete the item (the same values the product uses). _Required_ items must be answered before the mentor can sign and the placement can become active; optional items can be skipped. _Sources_ refer to the numbered list at the end.

## 1. Welcome and introductions

**Rationale.** A named mentor, a named back-up and a warm first day are the basis of supervised practice learning. The NMC supervision standards and the concept note both start from clear, known supervisors.

## 2. Ward layout, facilities and safety

**Rationale.** Students are exposed to fire, sharps and infection risks from the first shift. WHO standard precautions, the hand hygiene 'My 5 Moments' and the sharps and exposure guidance are the minimum safety orientation. Local policy always overrides this wording.

Note any areas a student must not enter alone.                                                                               | S2, S10 |
| 5   | Fire exits, assembly point, fire alarm and extinguisher locations shown.                                                            | Mentor                  | Yes      | Point to each exit and the assembly point. ## 4. Emergency preparedness

**Rationale.** The WHO Safe Childbirth Checklist is built on early recognition and calling for help. For a student the safe orientation is: know how to raise the alarm, where equipment is, what your role is, and where the written protocols live. No clinical protocol is reproduced here, by design.

| #   | Item                                                                                                                                                        | Who                     | Required | Guidance for the mentor                                                                                               | Sources |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| 16  | How to call for help shown: emergency bell or alarm, emergency phone number or extension, and who responds.                                                 | Mentor or nurse manager | Yes      | Have the student locate the alarm and say the number aloud. This is about raising the alarm, not clinical management. | S1, S2  |
| 17  | Location of emergency equipment shown (emergency trolley, oxygen, suction, resuscitation areas), with a reminder not to move or use it without supervision. | Mentor                  | Yes      | Orientation only. Equipment checks and use are taught later under supervision.                                        | S1, S2  |
| 18  | The student's role in an emergency explained: stay calm, call for help, follow instructions, stay within scope and make space.                              | Mentor                  | Yes      | Make clear that a student asking for help early is always the right action.                                           | S8, S9  |
| 19  | Where the unit's written emergency protocols are kept shown, and the student asked to read them with the mentor.                                            | Mentor or nurse manager | Yes      | Point to the facility's own documents. | 20  | Emergency drill or simulation arrangements explained, including how the student can take part or observe.                                                   | Nurse manager           | Optional | Share the date of the next drill if one is planned.                                                                   | S1, S10 |

**Rationale.** Labour wards are emotionally demanding and students are in a power-imbalanced position. Occupational health, debrief, and a safe route for concerns and feedback are standard parts of a supportive placement.

## Assumptions

- The audience is students on a labour-ward placement in a Kenyan facility; wording is plain English and can be translated later.
- Each facility has its own written emergency protocols, infection prevention and control policy, sharps and exposure pathway and waste policy. The checklist only checks that the student has been shown them.
- The Nursing Council of Kenya's specific student-practice rules and the Kenya Quality Model for Health maternity standards were referenced at a general level. The exact clauses were not extracted, so no item claims to meet a named clause.
- The mentor can complete almost every item. Items marked _Nurse manager_ or _Mentor or nurse manager_ assume the nurse manager is available for introductions, drills and escalation briefings. In the product, both mentors and nurse managers can record answers.
- The NMC standards are a UK document used here only as a model of good supervision (practice supervisor, regular feedback, raising concerns).
- Required items are those without which a student could be unsafe or unsupported on the first shift. This is a proposal and should be challenged.
- The 'first mentorship session' item stores the agreed date and time in the item note, as in the concept note. A mentor must enter it before marking the item done.

## Open questions for the clinical leads

- **Final wording.** Are the 40 items the right level of detail, or should some be merged or removed? Is anything essential missing for your ward?
- **Who completes what.** Which items should belong to the nurse manager rather than the mentor (for example drills, reporting lines, occupational health)? Should some items require the student too?
- **Required or optional.** Which items must block activation of a placement, and which are good practice only? Fewer required items make induction quicker; more make it safer.
- **Facility-specific items.** Does each facility need its own extras (for example theatre access, the neonatal unit, a specific escalation route, local languages)? One programme-wide checklist or one per facility?
- **Language.** Should the checklist also be available in Kiswahili, and who reviews the translation?
- **Emergency items.** Is the line between orientation (where is the alarm, what is my role) and training (what to do) in the right place?
- **Occupational health.** Is it appropriate to mention occupational health and vaccination in orientation, and who in the facility owns that process?
- **Regulator alignment.** Can the Nursing Council of Kenya or the Ministry of Health confirm any mandatory orientation content we should add?
